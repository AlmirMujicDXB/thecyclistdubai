The Cyclist Dubai Etiquette

How we ride together on Dubai tracks. These are the same rules as the Group Ride Etiquette Quiz, written out so you can read them before the ride.

Ride on the right

Stay on the right side of the track, whether you are alone or in a group. Leave the left side clear for faster riders. Do not sit in the middle because it feels more visible.

Why it matters. Dubai tracks pass on the left. A rider in the middle blocks the group behind and the people trying to come through.

Signals the group can see

The rider behind you cannot see the road you can see. Signal early, and say it as well as show it.

  • Right turnRight arm straight out. A left turn is the left arm straight out.
  • StopHand straight up. The group stops.
  • SlowingPat the air with your palm down.
  • HazardPoint down at the road and call “Hole” or “Hazard” for a pothole, sand or debris.
  • End of a pullSay “Rider change” and circle one hand, finger pointing down, as you rotate to the back.
  • Passing a riderSay “Passing left” or “On your left”, and point left.

Why it matters. A late signal is how a smooth group becomes a stack of wheels.

How the group rides

  • Single file on narrow bridges, through underpasses and past construction. Two abreast is for open track.
  • Leave 30–60 cm (about 1–2 feet) between your front wheel and the wheel ahead. Close enough to share the work, far enough to brake.
  • On Al Qudra, a group of 8–12 riders is the size that still hears every call.
  • Ride a road bike. Time-trial and triathlon bikes are a poor fit in a tight group: the position and the handling leave you less room to correct.
  • When a faster group is coming up behind, call “Passing from behind”.

Why it matters. Overlapping wheels and a group that cannot hear each other are the two usual causes of a pile-up.

Passing another group

Wait until the path is clear. Call “Passing”. Stay on the left until every rider in your group is past, then move back to the right. Do not dive back in as soon as the front rider is clear, and do not leave it to only the front rider to look.

Why it matters. Cutting back early pinches the last rider in your group, or the people you just passed.

Lights, helmet and a small kit

  • A helmet is mandatory on Dubai cycling tracks. The RTA rule is that riders must wear a protective helmet. It is not only for children, and not only at the weekend.
  • TheCyclistDubai rides: lights and a helmet, every time.
  • Early morning and late evening need a white light in front and a red light behind. A reflective vest on its own is not enough.
  • Carry a spare tube, a mini pump, tyre levers and a multitool. One of those four is what actually gets a flat rolling again.

Why it matters. Desert tracks are dark before sunrise, and a roadside stop without a tube ends the ride for everyone waiting.

Mushrif Park trails

  • Riders 12 and older may ride the MTB trails on their own. Younger children ride with an adult.
  • Use tyres 40 mm or wider. Slick 23–25 mm road tyres do not hold the dirt.

Why it matters. The surface is compact dirt, not tarmac, and a young rider out of sight of an adult has no one to call the next corner.

Phones and photos

Do not take a photo, use a selfie stick, or look at your phone while you are riding. Stop, move completely off the track, and then take the picture. A rest stop in the middle of the lane is still in the lane.

Why it matters. One hand on the bars and your eyes on a screen is how a quiet group ride meets a faster rider coming through.

These are etiquette, not the law

This page is how The Cyclist Dubai rides together. Official Dubai cycling rules and regulations are on the RTA cycling track page. Read those as well. This guide does not replace them.
